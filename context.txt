Cold storage represents a strategic challenge for the agricultural sector in Tunisia, yet its development remains uneven and largely insufficient, particularly at the farm level. Several studies, notably those conducted by the FAO and CIHEAM, indicate that postharvest losses of perishable products such as fruits, vegetables, potatoes, and dates can reach significant levels due to the lack or weakness of an efficient cold chain. These losses directly affect farmers’ incomes and national food security. For instance, in the potato sector, the absence of adequate storage infrastructure often forces farmers to sell immediately after harvest at low prices, which explains the growing interest in on-farm storage solutions to reduce losses and stabilize market supply (ArgenPapa, 2021). Although Tunisia has a network of cold storage facilities, these are mainly concentrated within industrial and logistics operators, making access difficult for small-scale farmers and thereby reinforcing value chain imbalances (Tunisie Industrie). Furthermore, existing refrigeration infrastructures are often energy-intensive and rely on conventional technologies, raising both economic and environmental concerns in the context of the ongoing ecological transition of the refrigeration sector (Challenges TN, 2024). However, some industrial initiatives, such as those developed by Horchani in the storage of fruits, vegetables, and dates, demonstrate the potential for improving product preservation through modern and optimized cold storage solutions. In parallel, scientific studies emphasize the importance of optimizing cold room design and operation, for example through CFD modeling, to ensure thermal uniformity and reduce quality degradation. Overall, cold storage in Tunisia lies at the intersection of multiple challenges: reducing postharvest losses, improving farmers’ income, enhancing access to infrastructure, and transitioning toward more sustainable energy systems. In this context, the development of decentralized, smart, and renewable energy-powered cold storage solutions appears to be a promising pathway, particularly for small and medium-scale farmers.
